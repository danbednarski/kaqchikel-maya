# Attested model sentences, by grammar topic

Every sentence below is quoted from a public or permission-cleared source, checked token by
token against that source, and cited. Imitate these rather than inventing
new patterns: find the section for the construction you need, copy its frame, and swap in
attested words. The note under each heading is ours; the sentences are the sources'.

Translation column: English when the source gives English, `es:` before the source's Spanish.
A translation we supplied ourselves ends in (our tr.); New Testament rows carry public-domain
King James (KJV) and Reina-Valera 1909 (RV1909) wording. Source translations are sometimes
free rather than literal; trust the Kaqchikel.

Sentences that Maxwell parsed carry his morpheme breakdown under the Kaqchikel, relabelled to the
usual convention: **Set A = ergative** (transitive subject, possessor), **Set B = absolutive**
(intransitive subject, transitive object, subject of a non-verbal predicate). His appendix uses A
for absolutive and E for ergative, the mirror image; never copy his letters. Abbreviations: COMPL
completive x-, INC incompletive n-/y-, POT potential xt-/xk-, IMP imperative t-/k-, 0 = zero 3sB,
AP antipassive, PASS passive, IV/TV intransitive/transitive suffix, PRF perfect, DET ri, IRR ta,
NEG man/ma, DAT -e (indirect object), NMLZ noun-forming suffix, FOC ja.

## Non-verbal predicates: no copula

Kaqchikel has no verb "to be". An adjective, noun or place of origin is the predicate by itself, and the subject is a Set B marker (in, at, öj, ïx, e; zero for 3rd singular) or just a following noun. Never insert a verb for "is/are". Negate with man ... ta around the predicate.

## Existence, location and "having": k'o, e k'o, majun, manäq

K'o means "there is", "is (located)" and, with a possessed noun, "have": k'o jun rutz'i' "she has a dog" is literally "there is her dog". The plural is e k'o. The negative is man k'o ta, or manäq / majun before the noun. Location needs k'o (or k'oje' outside the incompletive); it is never left out the way the copula is.

## Possession: prefixes and noun classes

A possessor is a Set A prefix on the noun: ru- (r- before a vowel), nu- (w-), a- (aw-), qa- (q-), i- (iw-), ki- (k-), and the possessor noun follows: rochoch ri Ixmukane "Ixmukane's house". Some nouns change when possessed: body parts and kin terms drop -aj or -(l)axel (jolomaj > rujolom), others add -il/-al/-el (b'aq > nub'aqil "my own bone"), and a lax final-syllable vowel becomes tense (tinamït > atinamit, tz'ib'ab'äl > nutz'ib'ab'al). Jay "house" has its own possessed stem: wochoch, rochoch.

## Intransitive verbs: aspect and person

The verb is built from aspect, then the Set B person marker, then the root. Completive x-, incompletive n- (3rd singular) or y- (other persons), potential xt- (3sg) or xk- (others): xiwär "I slept", yib'iyin "I walk", xkeb'e "they will go". The 3rd singular Set B is zero, so xb'e "she went", nb'e or nib'e "he goes". Lax vowels in the root stay lax: xiwär, not xiwar.

## Transitive verbs: aspect, Set A subject, Set B object

Order of pieces: aspect, the object's Set B marker, the subject's Set A marker, root. With a 3rd singular object the Set B slot is empty: x-0-u-löq' "she bought it", n-0-in-löq' "I buy it". The Set A 3rd singular is ru- (u- after a consonant-final prefix: xu-, nu-, xtu-), 3rd plural ki-. Root transitives lose their final vowel suffix in the completive and incompletive (xinwïl, ninlöq') but take -o'/-a'/-u' at the end of a phrase in the imperative and potential (tatija', xtiqatz'eta').

## Transitive verbs with 1st and 2nd person objects

When the object is not 3rd singular, its Set B marker comes right after the aspect: yatinq'etej "I hug you" is y-at-in-q'etej INC-2sB-1sA-hug; xkatinto' "I will help you" is xk-at-in-to'. A plural 3rd person object is e (y-e-, x-e-, xk-e-), often written with ' before a vowel-initial Set A prefix (ye'into' "I help them"). Do not drop the object marker or swap the two sets.

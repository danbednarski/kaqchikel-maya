# Dialects: how Kaqchikel varies from town to town

Kaqchikel is spoken in some fifty municipalities, and the towns differ in pronouns, aspect
prefixes, vowels, function words and everyday vocabulary. A model that writes one uniform
Kaqchikel is wrong for most speakers, and a model that mixes towns inside one sentence is wrong
for all of them. This file gives the evidence, then the rules for choosing a form.

The main evidence is unusual: Wycliffe translated the New Testament separately into seven
Kaqchikel varieties. The seven texts are aligned verse by verse (7947 verses present in all
seven), so the same sentence can be read in seven towns' speech. Every count below comes from
that alignment.

## Practical rules for a model (read this first)

1. Default to the ALMG standard (the form in grammar.md, lexicon.md and the Oxlajuj Aj and
   *Kik'aslemal ri Kaqchikela'* courses): rïn, rat, rija', röj, rïx, rije' (¿La ütz awäch? writes rin, rix; yïn for rïn is
   common in Tecpán, Comalapa and the east, and the Oxlajuj Aj course writes it too); incompletive y-/n-;
   3rd plural e-; wawe' "here"; ütz; winäq. It is understood everywhere and it is what schools,
   the Academy (ALMG) and published courses use.
2. Never mix varieties inside one text. If the user writes ren for "I", keep ren, and also use
   the Sacatepéquez-style jin-/jix-/je- prefixes rather than yin-; if they write inre', you are
   in Santa María de Jesús speech (nquin-, nca'-, i for 3rd plural, ie/uo vowels).
3. Label a local form instead of presenting it as the standard: "In Santa María de Jesús people
   say inre' for 'I' (standard rïn)." Give the standard form next to it.
4. When asked about a town not covered here, say so plainly. Then reason from the nearest
   covered town and the regional pattern (see the town guide below), and flag the answer as an
   expectation, not a fact: "Sumpango is not in these sources; nearby Xenacoj and Santiago
   Sacatepéquez use che re' for 'here', so you may hear that, but check with a speaker."
5. Spelling in the New Testament quotes below is the translators' own (pre-ALMG: c/qu = k,
   k = q, v = w, ü or e for ä, and e can also stand for ï, so Sacatepéquez ren is rïn and
   Yepocapa yen is yïn). Do not copy those spellings into modern writing; convert to ALMG
   and keep only the dialect choice (vova' -> wowa', nquin- -> nkin-, quinojiel -> kinojiel).
6. Spanish loans (discípulos, santo, cuando, pero, hermanos) are frequent in some of these
   translations by the translators' policy. Do not read them as evidence that a town lacks the
   Kaqchikel word.
7. A single verse proves nothing about dialect. Treat a difference as dialectal only when it is
   systematic: the alignment percentage in the table below says how often a variety uses its
   form in the verses where the central text uses the anchor form.

searching or comparing, but quote it verbatim. Its other spellings (c/qu, v) are pre-ALMG like
the rest.

## Town guide: what to expect where

Grounded in the NT counts and the OKMA survey pages cited; anything beyond that is marked as an
expectation.

- Antigua area, south (Santa María de Jesús): the most distinct variety near Antigua. Pronouns
  inre', atre', jaja', ojre', ixre', ijeje'; nk-/k- incompletive (survey p.53); 3rd plural i
  (p.46); diphthongs ie, uo (p.36); ja wa "this", ja re' "that" (pp.68-69); rumak "because"
  (p.44); muqul "cloud" (p.62). The NT column is your evidence.
- San Antonio Aguas Calientes, Santa Catarina Barahona, Ciudad Vieja: southern Sacatepéquez. The
  survey has Aguas Calientes (code ANT): wowe' "here" (p.64), nq- for 1st plural incompletive
  (p.49), nt- in the potential (p.54), ten vowels (p.32), achke/achike (p.69). Expect ren/raja'
  style pronouns like the Sacatepéquez Sur NT, but that NT is not certainly from Aguas Calientes.
- Sumpango, Santiago Sacatepéquez (Chi K'im), Santo Domingo Xenacoj: northern Sacatepéquez. Not
  in the NTs. The survey has Santa María Cauqué (Santiago Sacatepéquez municipality) and Xenacoj:
  che re' "here" (p.64), Xenacoj nd-/n- incompletive (pp.48, 53), Xenacoj ä drifting to e
  (wineq, p.36), Cauqué k- potential (p.54). Sumpango itself is in none of our sources: expect it
  to pattern with Xenacoj and Santiago, and say so.
- San Juan and San Pedro Sacatepéquez, Chuarrancho: the Eastern NT; nk- incompletive, xbij with
  no u-, chere'.
- Tecpán, Comalapa, Patzún, Patzicía, Poaquil: central; closest to the ALMG standard. Patzicía
  has nd-/d- incompletive (p.48) and re ye' "this" (p.68); Patzún drops y- before in- (p.52).
- Sololá and the lake: riyin-type pronouns; ya for ja; tek; rik'in worn to k'in (p.44).
- Yepocapa, Acatenango, Pochuta: nd- incompletive on intransitives (p.48); yen/yoj/yex pronouns;
  chin/chi for richin (p.45).

### Reading the table: the systematic patterns

- Pronouns form four families: yin/rat/rija' (central, Eastern, Chimaltenango), riyin/riyit/riya'
  (Sololá), ren/ret/raja' (Sacatepéquez Sur) with yen/reja' in Yepocapa, and the
  suffixed inre'/atre'/ojre' of Santa María de Jesús.
- The incompletive y- (yin-, yat-, yoj-, yix-, ye-) is j- in Sacatepéquez Sur (jin-, jat-, joj-,
  jix-, je-) and nk- in Eastern and SMJ (nquin-, ncat-, nkoj-, nquix-). SMJ's 3rd plural is nca'-
  and its completive 3rd plural xa'-.
- Yepocapa adds d after n- on intransitives (ndoc, ndel) but not on transitives.
- The b of b'ij "say" and b'än "do" disappears after a prefix in Sacatepéquez Sur, SMJ and
  Chimaltenango (xu'ij, xu'on, nu'ej), and Eastern drops the 3s Set A u- (xbij, xbün).
- SMJ diphthongizes tense e and o (nojiel, c'aslien, xuoc, ulief), and Yepocapa backs a to o in
  some words (rutota', vova', vocame).
- Function words with the most spread: "when" (tok, tek, cuando, antok, tak), "here" (vave',
  chere', wawe', we', vova'), "very" (janíla, juis, sibilaj, yalan, altíra, camas), "like"
  (achi'el, cachi'el, anche'l, inche'l, ancha'l), "what" (achique, anchique, andex, chica).
- Chimaltenango is a freer, meaning-based translation: its percentages run low everywhere
  (for example chuka' 2%), which says nothing about Chimaltenango speech. Sololá often prints
  an alternate in parentheses (riche (rixin), xaxu (xaxe wi)), so both forms count there.
- Translator choice, not dialect: disciples (tijoxela' vs discípulos), holy (lok'olej vs santo),
  brothers (kach'alal vs hermanos), "when" = cuando in Sacatepéquez Sur, numbers written in
  Spanish (doce, siete). These track each translation's loan policy.

## Limits of this evidence

- Seven translations, each by one team, mostly from the 1990s to 2012. A translation is
  careful, formal, often conservative speech, not a recording of a market conversation.
- All seven NTs are pre-ALMG. They show which vowel a variety uses relative to the others, but
  never cite them as the authority on a word's exact vowel quality.
- Antigua-area towns not covered by either the NTs or the survey (Sumpango, Ciudad Vieja, San
  Miguel Dueñas, Alotenango, Santa Catarina Barahona, Pastores, Jocotenango): answer from the
  nearest covered town and say it is an inference.
- Alignment percentages are lower bounds: a verse can be rephrased so the word never appears
  in one translation, which lowers the percentage without meaning the variety lacks the form.

# Kaqchikel working grammar, by topic

This is a working grammar for writing and checking Kaqchikel. Each topic opens with the mistake
models make most often, then states the rule, gives the smallest useful paradigm, and shows it
working in sentences quoted from sources that allow quotation. Full tables are in
`paradigms.md`.

Sources quoted here (short selections, with permission or under open licences):
- `[rukan p.N]`: Little, Maxwell, Cuma Chávez & Cuma Chávez, *Kik'aslemal ri Kaqchikela' /
  Kaqchikel Maya Life* (unpublished draft; quoted with the authors' permission). Its Appendix B
  (pp. 201-302) is a modern grammar by Judith Maxwell. `[rukan_interlinear p.N n.M]` is example
  M on page N of that appendix.
- `[oxlajuj p.N]`: Ixq'anil (Maxwell) & Waqi' Kan (Little), *Kaqchikel Tijonïk: Oxlajuj Aj*,
  course of the Oxlajuj Aj summer program (quoted with permission; N = PDF page). Its
  explanations are in Spanish, so its examples come with Spanish glosses.
- `[bloom: ...]`, `[wiki: ...]`: Bloom Library readers and the Kaqchikel Wikipedia test wiki
  (CC BY-NC-SA).

Sources cited but never quoted (counting and page references only): *¿La ütz awäch?
Introduction to Kaqchikel Maya Language* (Brown, Maxwell & Little 2006), cited "La ütz awäch?
p.N"; and the OKMA reference grammar, García Matzar & Rodríguez Guaján, *Rukemik ri Kaqchikel
Chi' / Gramática Kaqchikel* (1997), cited "OKMA Gramática scan p.N" (scan page, not printed page).

Labels: Set A = ergative (it names the doer of a two-participant verb and the owner of a noun).
Set B = absolutive (it names the one participant of a one-participant verb or of a non-verbal
predicate, and the undergoer of a two-participant verb). The
sequel's own analysis lines use A = absolutive and E = ergative, the mirror image; translate
before comparing. Oxlajuj Aj uses Juego A = ergativo and Juego B = absolutivo, same as here.
Glosses in English and Spanish come from the source unless marked "(our tr.)".

## 1. Sounds and spelling

**Models get wrong:** dropping the lax vowels (writing chabal for ch'ab'äl, tinamit for
tinamït); writing them in the wrong place (a lax vowel anywhere but the last syllable); leaving
a lax vowel in place after a suffix; writing b instead of b'; curly quotes for the glottal stop;
writing a glottal stop at the start of a word.

**Alphabet (ALMG).** Vowels: a, e, i, o, u (tense) and ä, ë, ï, ö, ü (lax). Consonants:
b', ch, ch', j, k, k', l, m, n, p, q, q', r, s, t, t', tz, tz', w, x, y, and the glottal stop
'. The glottal stop is a full consonant, typed as a straight apostrophe. Word-initially it is
left unwritten, yet still pronounced: "vowel-initial" roots are really glottal-initial, and the
glottal reappears after the 3p prefix (xe'el 'they left') [rukan p.205, p.212 fn.6]. k is
velar, q is uvular; k' q' t' tz' ch' b' are glottalized. x is English "sh". j is soft, like
English h. b' is implosive and is the only b in native words.

**Vowels.** Five tense vowels a e i o u in every town, plus up to five lax vowels ä ë ï ö ü.
"The lax vowels in descending order of frequency of use within different communities are ä > ü >
ï > ö > ë" [rukan p.205]; ë is consistently produced only in San José Poaquil, and ä ranges from
[ɛ] to schwa to [ɯ] by town [rukan p.205]. Write the lax vowels wherever the standard has them
even if a local accent merges them: they carry grammar.

**The lax-vowel rule.** "In Kaqchikel lax vowels can only exist in final syllables" [rukan
p.206]. So a lax vowel becomes tense whenever a suffix pushes it out of the last syllable:

| base | suffixed | gloss | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| ixöq | ixoqi' | woman, women | [rukan p.207] |
| xtän | xtani' | girl, girls | [rukan p.207] |
| umül | umuli' | rabbit, rabbits | [rukan p.207] |
| xinlöq' | nuloq'on | I bought it / I have bought it | [rukan p.208] |
| xatintz'ët | nutz'eton | I saw you / I've seen it | [rukan p.208] |
| ninkëm | takema' | lo tejo / téjelo | [oxlajuj p.37] |
| ninjäl | tajala' | lo cambio / cámbielo | [oxlajuj p.37] |

A second kind of tensing happens with no suffix at all: class-2 nouns tense their last vowel when
possessed (tinamït > nutinamit, tz'ib'ab'äl > nutz'ib'ab'al, tijonïk > nutijonik) [rukan
p.207]. And the reverse: a class-3 noun that drops its suffix can expose a lax vowel (wachaj >
nuwäch 'my face', aqanaj > waqän 'my leg') [rukan p.207].

Rules of thumb for where lax vowels sit, from the sequel [rukan pp.211-212]: (1) root
transitives have lax vowels unless they end in a glottal stop (-b'än, -löq', but -to', -tzu');
(2) positional statives have them (tz'uyül, jeb'ël, simïl); (3) nouns in -b'äl and -ïk have them
unpossessed and lose them possessed (tijob'äl > nutijob'al; tijonïk > nutijonik), except
ch'ab'äl 'language', which never tenses.

**Other regular sound facts.**
- b' ~ ' alternation: a final glottal stop can come back as b' before a suffix: juna' 'year' >
  junab'ïr 'last year', wuqu' 'seven' > wuqub'ix 'in a week', ka'i' 'two' > kab'lajuj
  'twelve' [rukan p.209].
- Epenthetic i: speakers break up prefix clusters with i: nb'iyin ~ nib'iyin 's/he walks',
  xtb'iyin ~ xtib'iyin [rukan p.210]. Both spellings occur; the i-form is more common in print.
- Final m is often pronounced n (nupam ~ nupan 'my stomach'); the standard writes m [rukan
  pp.210-211].
- Final l, r, w, y are voiceless [rukan pp.206].

Also: La ütz awäch? pp.8-12, 144-145; OKMA Gramática scan pp.30-32 (vocales relajadas).

**Drift.** The sequel applies word-final laxing more widely than the 2006 course: rïn, ïn, rïx,
iwïr, wakamïn, chïk, töq where the 2006 course and many schoolbooks write rin, in, rix, iwir,
wakami, chik, toq. Both are in current print; pick one system per text. The 2006 course counts
34 sounds, the sequel 22 consonants + 10 vowels = 32 [rukan p.205].

## 2. Set A and Set B: the two person-marker sets

**Models get wrong:** using the possessive prefix nu- as the subject of a verb (*xnub'än* for
xinb'än 'I did it'); using Set A on an intransitive verb (*xinwär* for xiwär 'I slept'); treating
the 3s Set B as "nothing happens" rather than as a zero that triggers n-/xt-.

**Rule.** Kaqchikel is ergative: the lone participant of a one-participant verb is marked
exactly like the undergoer of a two-participant verb (Set B, absolutive), while the doer of a
two-participant verb is marked exactly like a noun's owner (Set A, ergative). "The subject of Kaqchikel intransitive
verbs is marked by the absolutive set of pronouns. ... The subject of Kaqchikel transitive verbs
is marked by the ergative set of pronouns" [rukan p.212].

| person | Set B before C / V | Set A on verbs, before C / V | Set A on nouns, before C / V |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1s | i- / in- | in- / inw- | nu- / w- |
| 2s | a- / at- | a- / aw- | a- / aw- |
| 3s | zero | (r)u- / r- | ru- / r- |
| 1p | oj- | qa- / q- | qa- / q- |
| 2p | ix- | i- / iw- | i- / iw- |
| 3p | e- / e'- | ki- / k- | ki- / k- |

The only difference between verbal and nominal Set A is the first person singular: in-/inw- on
verbs, nu-/w- on nouns, relational nouns and perfects ("the preverbal ergative first-person
pronouns here are //-in-// before consonants and //inw-// before vowels, while the possessive
ergative prefixes on nouns are //nu-// before consonants and //w-// before vowels" [rukan
p.214]).

Set B 1s and 2s before a consonant: the default is i- and a-, which both courses teach and texts
overwhelmingly write (xib'e 'I went' 47 tokens vs xinb'e 0; xkib'e 'I will go' 40 vs xkinb'e 1,
the latter only in the OKMA grammar). in- and at- before a consonant do occur as a minority,
regional form in the OKMA grammar and some school readers (xatb'e, yatb'e, xinwa'). Use i-/a-
unless you are matching a source that keeps the n/t.

Examples:
- Xojsamäj. 'We worked.' (x-oj-samäj, Set B subject) [rukan_interlinear p.212 n.1]
- Xqab'än qasamaj. 'We did our work.' (x-0-qa-b'än, Set A subject; qa- possessor) [rukan_interlinear p.212 n.2]
- xinsik'ij 'I read it' / nuwuj 'my book'; xinwoyoj 'I called her/him' / woj 'my avocado' [rukan p.213]
- yatinq'etej 'yo te abrazo'; yinaq'etej 'tú me abrazas'; yiruq'etej 'él me abraza' [oxlajuj p.39]

Also: La ütz awäch? pp.61, 85; OKMA Gramática scan pp.41, 48-49.

## 3. The verb template

**Models get wrong:** putting the object after the subject marker; inventing tense suffixes;
forgetting that the object comes first inside the word.

**Rule.** "the formula for the transitive verb is TAM + absolutive + ergative + verb" [rukan
p.214]; for an intransitive, TAM + absolutive + verb. Oxlajuj Aj states it as "TAM + Juego B +
Juego A + Verbo" [oxlajuj p.39]. Optional suffixes follow the stem: status suffix -V' on root
transitives in the imperative, voice suffixes, -näq/-on perfects, and the rare aspect suffixes
-xta- 'quickly' and -(V)la' 'repeatedly'.

| slot | 1 aspect | 2 Set B | 3 Set A | 4 stem | 5 suffix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| xatinq'ejela' 'I greeted you' | x- | at- | in- | q'ejela' | |
| xerupeyoj 's/he invited them' | x- | e- | ru- | peyoj | |
| tab'ana' 'do it!' | t- | 0 | a- | b'än > b'an | -a' |

(forms from [rukan p.214] and [oxlajuj p.36])

Rare suffixes: -xta- "rapid action", followed by -n on intransitives and -j on transitives:
Xelxtan ri xtän. 'The girl left quickly.' [rukan_interlinear p.216 n.14]; "This suffix is common
in Chi Xot (Chiq'a'l, San Juan Comalapa) and Pwaqil (San José Pwaqil)" [rukan p.216]. -(V)la'
marks many recipients or repetition: Xe'intola' ri tijoxela'. 'I helped the students
repeatedly. OR I helped a lot of students.' [rukan p.217]

## 4. Aspect: the n-/y-, x-, xt-/xk-, t-/k- prefixes

**Models get wrong:** treating Kaqchikel as having past/present/future tense endings; using y-
before a 3s subject (*yb'e*) or n- before a non-3s one (*nib'e* for 'I go'); writing *xkb'e* or
*xtib'e* for 'I will go' (it is xkib'e); forgetting that every finite verb needs a prefix.

**Rule.** Each aspect prefix has two shapes, chosen by the Set B that follows: one before the
zero 3s Set B, one before every other Set B (whether that Set B is an intransitive subject or a
transitive object).

| aspect | before zero 3s | before other Set B | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| incompletive | n- / ni- | y- | present, habitual, ongoing, near future |
| completive | x- | x- | finished event, usually past |
| potential | xt- / xti- | xk- | projected, future |
| imperative | t- / ti- | k- | command, wish, "let's", "may he" |

"The form //y-// occurs before all the absolutive pronouns (whether intransitive subjects or
transitive objects) except third person singular ... Before this zero morpheme, the present
incompletive marker becomes //n-//" [rukan p.215]. Oxlajuj Aj says the same in Spanish: "El
marcador de tiempo “presente” es y con todas las personas menos tercera persona singular, y n
con tercera persona singular" [oxlajuj p.23]. The potential: "one before the third person
singular absolutive //xt-//, another before all other absolutives ... //xk-//" [rukan p.214].

-b'e 'go / ir', incompletive [oxlajuj p.24]: Rïn yib'e. 'Yo voy.' / Rat yab'e. 'Tú vas.' /
Rija' nb'e. 'El/ella va.' / Röj yojb'e. / Rïx yixb'e / Rije' yeb'e.

Examples:
- Yib'iyin jun ramaj pa taq nimaq'a'. 'I walk an hour in the mornings.' [rukan_interlinear p.215 n.1]
- Achike nab'än wakamïn? Ninch'äj nutzyaq. 'What are you doing? I'm washing my clothes.' [rukan_interlinear p.215 n.2]
- Iwïr toq'a' jeb'ël xiwär. 'I slept well last night.' [rukan_interlinear p.215 n.4]
- Xkeb'e pa ruchi' palow. 'They will go to the seashore.' [rukan_interlinear p.216 n.8]
- Xtik'ule' ri juna' apo. 'He'll marry next year.' [rukan_interlinear p.216 n.10]

The incompletive covers English present, progressive, habitual and near future: the sequel
calls it "the most elastic of the TAM markers" [rukan p.215]. To stress an ongoing action,
speakers add the auxiliary tajin before an incompletive verb: tajin nkäm qa 'is dying out'
[wiki: E oxi' q'opoji' aj Tz'oloj Ya']; the sequel's grammar names the helper verb -ajin
[rukan p.214].

Also: La ütz awäch? pp.166-171; OKMA Gramática scan pp.50, 389-396.

**Variation.** ni- vs n- before a consonant (nib'e / nb'e) is a spelling and dialect choice; both
are well attested. In some towns a monosyllabic vowel-initial verb hardens the prefix: "se puede
escuchar que se diga nel, ntel, ndel (él sale)" [oxlajuj p.23].

## 5. Third person singular Set A: u- / r- / ru-

**Models get wrong:** *xrub'än* for xub'än 'she did it'; *xub'än* style u- after an object
prefix (*xeub'än*); *xuoyoj* for xroyoj.

**Rule.** Before a consonant stem, 3s Set A is ru-, but its r drops right after an aspect prefix
(x-, n-, xt-, t-), leaving u-. After a Set B prefix (any non-3s object) the r stays. Before a
vowel stem it is r- everywhere.

- xusik'ij 's/he read it': "This form comes from //x-// 'past' +//0// 3rd person singular
  absolutive + //-ru-// 3rd person singular ergative + sik'ij ... The /r/ of //-ru-// disappears
  after the //x-// tense-aspect-mood marker" [rukan p.213 fn.7]
- xroyoj 'S/he called her/him.' (vowel stem) [rukan p.213]
- xerupeyoj 'S/he invited them' (after object e-) [rukan p.214]
- Ri ajyuq' xeruyuq'uj rukarne'l. 'The shepherd herds his sheep.' [rukan_interlinear p.237 n.12]
- xarutöj 'la pagó a Ud.' [oxlajuj p.79]

The same r appears in the potential and imperative before vowels: 'she will find it' is written
both xtirïl (8 tokens) and xtrïl (3) (paradigms 4c). On nouns there is no aspect prefix, so the possessor is always ru-/r-: ruwuj,
rochoch.

## 6. Transitive verbs: root vs derived

**Models get wrong:** adding -j to root verbs (*xulöq'aj*); dropping the -j of derived verbs
(*xutz'ib'*); writing the root vowel tense in the plain form (*xuloq'* for xulöq'); carrying
the imperative -V' into other aspects (*xulöq'o'*); using the wrong passive or antipassive for the class.

**Rule.** Two classes of transitive verb. Root transitives are a single CVC root (-b'än 'do',
-löq' 'buy', -tz'ët 'see', -ïl 'find', -to' 'help'). Their vowel is lax when the root ends the
word, unless the root ends in a glottal stop. Derived transitives end in -Vj (-tz'ib'aj 'write',
-k'utuj 'ask', -oyob'ej 'wait for', -q'etej 'hug'). "Los radicales consisten en una raíz (más sus
flexiones). Los derivados ... terminan en sufijo: -Vj" [oxlajuj p.87]. The class decides four
things:

| | root transitive | derived transitive |
|---|---|---|
| 2s imperative | -V' added, vowel tenses: tab'ana' | unchanged: tatz'ib'aj |
| passive | vowel tenses: xloq', xtz'et (some speakers add -Vx) | -Vj > -Vx (laxed): xtz'ib'äx |
| antipassive | -on / -un: xloq'on | -Vj > -Vn: xtz'ib'an |
| perfect | -on / -un: nuloq'on | -Vj > -Vn: rutz'ib'an |

Examples:
- Xinb'än ri samaj. 'I did the work.' / Xb'an ri samaj. 'The work was done.' [rukan p.208]
- Xintz'ib'aj ri wuj 'I wrote the book' / Xtz'ib'äx ri wuj 'The book was written' [rukan p.221]
- -tz'ib'aj 'Escribir' > -tz'ib'an; -q'etej 'abrazar' > -q'eten; -k'utuj 'preguntar' > -k'utun [oxlajuj p.87]

Also: La ütz awäch? pp.174-183; OKMA Gramática scan pp.299-310.

## 7. Imperatives and hortatives

**Models get wrong:** using the bare stem as a command (*b'än!*); forgetting the -V' on root
transitives (*tab'än*); putting -V' on derived ones (*tatz'ib'aja'*); using t- with a 1s object
(*tinato'* for kinato' 'help me'); negating with man ... ta plus the imperative prefix in all
cases.

**Rule.** The imperative/hortative prefix is k- before any non-zero Set B and t- before the zero
3s: "Es /k/ delante todas las personas gramaticales menos tercera persona singular del Juego B.
Es /t/ delante del la tercera persona singular" [oxlajuj p.36]. Intransitive commands: k- + Set
B (katel 'leave!', kixb'e 'go! (pl.)', kojb'e 'let's go'). Transitive with a 3s object: t- +
Set A (tab'ij 'say it!'). Root transitives add a harmonic vowel plus glottal stop: -a' after a,
e, i; -o' after o; -u' after u; roots already ending in ' add nothing: "Una vocal no redondeada
en la raíz, o sea /i, e, a/ toma /a/ como sufijo. Una vocal redondeada toma una copia como el
sufijo" [oxlajuj p.37].

| | Kaqchikel | Spanish (source) |
|---|---|---|
| root, 2s | tawila' | Adiviná. Adivine. [oxlajuj p.36] |
| root, 2s | tatz'eta' | Véalo. [oxlajuj p.36] |
| root, 2s | tachoyo' | Tálelo. [oxlajuj p.36] |
| root, 2s | tak'uyu' | Perdone. [oxlajuj p.36] |
| root, 1s object | kinak'uyu' | Perdóname. [oxlajuj p.36] |
| glottal root | kinato' | Ayudame. [oxlajuj p.36] |
| derived, 1p object | kojiwoyob'ej | Espérennos. [oxlajuj p.36] |
| 2p, 3s object | titzu' | Miren. [oxlajuj p.36] |

More examples:
- Tab'ij pe chwe. 'Tell me.' [rukan_interlinear p.255 n.30]
- Tab'ana' utzil, tatzija' ri q'aq'al. 'Please, turn on the light.' [rukan_interlinear p.272 n.50]
- Katampe', ala'. Kojb'e pa qatiko'n. (our tr.: 'Come here, boy. Let's go to our field.') [rukan p.55]
- Takuyu' numak. 'perdón, perdóname' [oxlajuj p.13]

**Negative commands.** Oxlajuj Aj notes that man ... ta with the imperative "suena algo mal"
and that speakers say man yatel ta, or reinforce with mani: Mani yatel. 'Que no salga.'; Man tab'ij
ke ri'. 'No digas eso. De nada.' [oxlajuj p.16]. The sequel: Mani nachäp! Tz'il. 'Don't touch
that! It's dirty.' [rukan_interlinear p.280 n.64]; Man katzaq pa b'ey! 'Take care (parting
salutation). Literally, don't fall in the road.' [rukan_interlinear p.279 n.61]. Biblical
prohibitions use man + imperative: Man takamisaj jun winäq. 'Thou shalt not kill.'
[rukan_interlinear p.279 n.57].

**Drift.** The sequel's appendix treats mani as child speech [rukan p.280], but adult speakers
use it freely in both books' corpora. The 2s object before a 1p/3p subject keeps at- in the
sequel and reduces in the 2006 course (see section 9).

Also: La ütz awäch? p.171; OKMA Gramática scan pp.114-116.

## 8. Non-verbal predicates: no copula

**Models get wrong:** inventing a verb "to be" (there is none for identity or quality);
borrowing Spanish es/está; using k'o for identity ("I am a teacher" is not *in k'o tijonel*);
putting a Set B prefix on a noun as if it were a verb.

**Rule.** A noun, adjective, positional stative or perfect can be a predicate by itself. Its
subject is a Set B form written as a separate word before it: ïn (in), at, (zero), öj, ïx, e.
The 3s is zero, so the free pronoun rija' often appears to make the subject audible. The sequel lists
nouns, adjectives and k'ojlemal (positionals) as predicates of this kind [rukan p.208].

| Kaqchikel | English | source |
|---|---|---|
| Ïn tijonel | I am a teacher | [rukan p.208] |
| Öj tijonela'. | We are teachers. | [rukan p.208] |
| At b'aq | You are slender. | [rukan p.208] |
| E jeb'ël. | They are pretty. | [rukan p.208] |
| Öj aj Pa Jotöl. | We are gringos. | [rukan_interlinear p.282 n.20] |
| Rija' b'ixanel. | She's a singer. | [rukan_interlinear p.282 n.23] |
| Jotöl rajïl. | It's expensive. Literally, high its-price. | [rukan_interlinear p.270 n.3] |

Oxlajuj Aj's paradigm: "Rïn in. . . Yo soy. . . / Rat at. . . Tú eres. . . / Rija'. . . El/ella
son. . . / Röj öj. . . / Rïx ïx. . . / Rije' e. . ." [oxlajuj p.19]. Negation wraps the
predicate: Rija' man tijonel ta. 'Ella no es maestra.' [oxlajuj p.16].

**Location and existence use k'o** (section 10), and "become" uses a verb (-ok 'enter, become':
Ri q'oraläj achin xok k'uch. 'The very lazy man turned into a buzzard.' [rukan_interlinear
p.262 n.3]). Identity in focus uses ja: Chwa'q ja nuq'ij. 'Tomorrow is my birthday.'
[rukan_interlinear p.248 n.20].

Also: La ütz awäch? pp.17-20, 61; OKMA Gramática scan p.51.

## 9. The transitive matrix: objects other than "it"

**Models get wrong:** putting the object marker after the subject marker; reversing the roles
(reading xinato' as 'I helped you'; it means 'You helped me' [rukan p.214]); using Set A for objects.

**Rule.** Object (Set B) first, then subject (Set A). Before a consonant (including a
consonant-initial Set A like ru-, qa-, ki-), 1s in- and 2s at- usually reduce to i- and a-, as the
courses teach (in-/at- kept there is a minority, regional form; see section 2); 3p e-
takes a glottal stop before a vowel-initial Set A.

| Kaqchikel | gloss | source |
|---|---|---|
| xatinq'ejela' | I greeted you | [rukan p.214] |
| xatinwoyob'ej | I called you. | [rukan p.214] |
| xinato' | You helped me. | [rukan p.214] |
| xerak'axaj | S/he heard them. | [rukan p.214] |
| xojiq'etej | Y'all hugged us. | [rukan p.214] |
| xekelesaj | They removed them. | [rukan p.214] |
| yiruq'etej | él me abraza | [oxlajuj p.39] |
| yaruq'etej | ella te abraza | [oxlajuj p.39] |
| yixqaq'etej | nosotros les abrazamos a Udes. | [oxlajuj p.39] |
| ye'iq'etej | vosotros les abrazáis | [oxlajuj p.39] |
| xe'inchajij | les cuidé | [oxlajuj p.79] |
| xinito' | me ayudaron Udes. | [oxlajuj p.79] |

If the doer and the undergoer are one person, switch to the reflexive (section 22): *xinintz'ët* does
not exist.

**Drift: 2s object before a plural subject.** The sequel says that at- "does
not reduce to /a/ even though it is followed by a consonant" when qa- or ki- follows, while
"//-ru-// however does" [rukan p.214 fn.8]: xatqacha' 'We chose
you' [rukan p.214]. Oxlajuj Aj reduces before ki-: yakiq'etej 'ellas te abrazan' [oxlajuj p.39];
La ütz awäch? p.86 reduces before both. The corpus counts favour the full form
(yatqato' 22 tokens vs yaqato' 1; yatkito' 31 vs yakito' 1), but most of those tokens come from
the Firefox strings (20 of the 22, 25 of the 31), so the evidence is thinner than it looks. Both
are acceptable; before ru- reduce: yaru-/xaru-.

## 10. Existence, location and having: k'o

**Models get wrong:** using a "have" verb (Kaqchikel has none); *manäq k'o* or *manäq pa
wochoch* for 'I'm not at home'; forgetting the plural e k'o; using k'o for identity.

**Rule.** k'o is an existential predicate: 'there is', 'is located', and with a possessed noun
'has'. Its subject takes Set B as a separate word before it (e k'o 'they are there'). In the past and
future it is the verb -k'oje': xk'oje', xkik'oje'. "Having" = k'o + possessed noun. The
negative existential is manäq (or majun 'none, no one'); negated location uses man ... k'o ta.

| Kaqchikel | English | source |
|---|---|---|
| K'o jun nuchaq'. | I have a younger same-sex sibling. | [rukan_interlinear p.278 n.32] |
| Manäq nuchaq', manäq nunimal, manäq nuxib'al. | (I have no siblings) | [rukan_interlinear p.278 n.33] |
| Ïn k'o pa wochoch. | I'm at home. | [rukan_interlinear p.279 n.36] |
| Man ïn k'o ta pa wochoch. | I'm not at home. | [rukan_interlinear p.279 n.37] |
| E k'o k'ïy umuli' pa ichaj. | There are many rabbits in the garden. | [rukan_interlinear p.279 n.40] |
| Manäq k'ïy umuli' pa ichaj. | There aren't many rabbits in the garden. | [rukan_interlinear p.279 n.41] |
| Xk'oje' jun nuwakx. Xa ja ri', xeleq'äx. | I had a cow. But it was stolen. | [rukan_interlinear p.279 n.44] |
| Xkik'oje' pa k'astanib'äl chwa'q. | I'll be in the park tomorrow. | [rukan_interlinear p.283 n.35] |
| Manjun k'o ta. | Nadie está. | [oxlajuj p.16] |

The sequel stars *In manäq pa wochoch* and *Manäq pa ruwi' ri ch'atal* as ungrammatical
[rukan_interlinear p.279 n.37, n.39]: manäq negates existence, not location.

"Need": nik'atzin (chi) 'it is necessary': Nik'atzin chi xtiqachajij ri awän. (our tr.: 'We need
to look after the cornfield.') [rukan p.55]. "Must": k'o chi + incompletive (section 18).

Also: La ütz awäch? pp.24-27, 190; OKMA Gramática scan p.51.

## 11. Possession and noun classes

**Models get wrong:** possessing jay 'house' (*nujay*; it is wochoch); keeping the unpossessed
suffix on body parts and kin (*nute'ej*, *nuwi'aj*); forgetting vowel tensing (*nutinamït*);
using w- before a consonant or nu- before a vowel; *rachoch* vs rochoch confusion.

**Rule.** The possessor is a Set A prefix (nu-, a-, ru-, qa-, i-, ki- before consonants; w-, aw-, r-, q-, iw-, k- before vowels); a
full-noun possessor follows the possessed noun: rochoch ri Ya Ixmukane 'Ixmukane's house'
[rukan_interlinear p.236 n.4]. Oxlajuj Aj: "Los posesivos que preceden a vocales son: w-, aw-,
r-, q-, iw-, k-" and before consonants nu-, a-, ru-, qa-, i-, ki- [oxlajuj p.10]. Nouns fall
into classes by what happens when possessed [rukan pp.241-247]:

| class | change | examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | none (the open class; loans and new words) | wuj > nuwuj; peqës > rupeqës; uk'ya' > quk'ya' [rukan p.241] |
| 2 | last lax vowel tenses | ch'akäch > ich'akach; jäl > kijal; wäy > away; tijob'äl > rutijob'al [rukan pp.241-242] |
| 3 | unpossessed suffix (-aj, -ej, -el, ...) drops | te'ej > qate'; tata'aj > itata'; wi'aj > nuwi'; pamaj > qapam [rukan pp.243-244] |
| 4 | adds -Vl when part of the body | b'aq > nub'aqil; kïk' > rukik'el [rukan p.246] |
| 5 | always possessed (relational nouns, -i' 'self') | -uk'in, -ichin, -e [rukan p.246] |
| suppletive | different root | jay > wochoch; k'ul > nuq'u' [rukan p.247] |

Class 4 is about the body: "nub'aqil is 'my bone, within my body'", but a bone you own is
nub'aq [rukan p.246]. Class membership shifts over time: 'face' palajaj ~ nupaläj "has become
simply paläj ~ nupaläj" [rukan p.245].

Examples with Spanish: -ochoch 'casa (forma poseída)': Wochoch 'mi casa', Rochoch 'su casa (de
él/ella)', qochoch 'nuestra casa' [oxlajuj p.10]; tinamït 'pueblo': nutinamit 'mi pueblo'
[oxlajuj p.10].

"Mine": wichin (relational -ichin): La q'ijob'äl la' wichin. 'That watch is mine.'
[rukan_interlinear p.247 n.17]; Man wichin ta. 'It's not mine.' [rukan_interlinear p.277 n.1].

**Drift.** rochoch vs rachoch: the sequel writes rochoch throughout; Oxlajuj Aj leans rachoch.
Teach rochoch, accept rachoch.

Also: La ütz awäch? pp.144, 154-155; OKMA Gramática scan pp.41-48.

## 12. Determiners and demonstratives

**Models get wrong:** dropping ri everywhere (or using it as Spanish "el" before every noun
including vocatives); writing "this" as re alone after the noun; forgetting the closing re' / la'.

**Rule.** ri marks a known, identified noun (much like 'the') and also sets off relative clauses
(section 25). Demonstratives are frames around the noun phrase: re ... re' 'this', la ... la'
'that'. "Se usa re. . re' para cosas cercanas" and "Se usa la. . la' para cosas lejanas" [oxlajuj p.18].
Alone they fuse: rere' 'ésto', lala' 'eso' [oxlajuj p.18]. Names take ri plus a title (ri xta
Mari'y, ri a Xwan, ri Ma Kawoq), but not in direct address [rukan p.236].

| Kaqchikel | gloss | source |
|---|---|---|
| Re wuj re' richin Ma B'eleje' Ey. | Este libro es del Sr. B'eleje' Ey. | [oxlajuj p.18] |
| La tz'ib'ab'äl la' q'ëq rub'onil. | Ese lápiz es negro. | [oxlajuj p.18] |
| La xtän la' jeb'ël nikemon. | That young woman weaves well. | [rukan_interlinear p.223 n.38] |
| Re ala' re' nikarun ruk'in rutata'. | This lad fishes with his father. | [rukan_interlinear p.223 n.39] |
| Ri xta Mari'y xel jukumaj. | Mary left early. | [rukan p.236] |

Fixed uses: ke re' / ke ri' 'like this / like that, so'; ruma ri' 'por eso' [oxlajuj p.18];
chi la' 'allí' [oxlajuj p.18]. Indefinite 'a, one' is jun: Xusipaj jun kab' chwe. 'She gave a
candy to me.' [rukan_interlinear p.247 n.18].

Also: La ütz awäch? pp.142, 157-158; OKMA Gramática scan pp.69-73.

## 13. Negation

**Models get wrong:** man without ta (or ta without man); putting ta right after man instead of
after the negated word; using manäq to negate a verb; *man xib'e* as a complete negative in
standard writing.

**Rule.** man ... ta brackets what is negated: man before the predicate, ta after the verb or
predicate word (before directionals and the rest of the clause). "La negación más común es man
. . . ta" [oxlajuj p.16]. Negative words carry negation by themselves and may still take ta:
manäq 'no, there is none', majun / manjun 'none, no one', majun b'ey 'never', majani 'not yet'.

| Kaqchikel | gloss | source |
|---|---|---|
| Man xixajon ta. | No bailé. | [oxlajuj p.16] |
| Man nïm ta ri atinamit. | Tu pueblo no es grande. | [oxlajuj p.16] |
| Man xitikïr ta yinel jukumaj. | I couldn't leave early. | [rukan_interlinear p.277 n.4] |
| Man xuya' ta apo chre ruchaq'. | She didn't give it to her younger sister. | [rukan_interlinear p.277 n.8] |
| Man ïn warinäq ta. | I'm not asleep. | [rukan_interlinear p.218 n.20] |
| Man ja ta ri aj Brazil ri xech'akon. | It wasn't the Brazilians who won. | [rukan_interlinear p.277 n.18] |
| Majun b'ey nutijon ruti'j o'on. | I've never eaten iguana. | [rukan_interlinear p.279 n.50] |
| Majani xiyakatäj, töq xpe ri kab'raqän. | I wasn't up yet, when the earthquake struck. | [rukan_interlinear p.279 n.54] |
| Manjun b'ey xintz'ët. | Nunca lo ví. | [oxlajuj p.16] |

Short answers: Manäq. 'No.'; Manäq matyöx. 'No thank you.' [rukan_interlinear p.278 n.28].
With a directional, ta comes first: Man xpe ta ri tijoxel. 'The student didn't come.' [rukan
p.230]; Man xub'ij ta pe chwe ... [rukan_interlinear p.230 n.70].

Counterfactual manta: Manta xab'ij pe chwe, manta xinwetamaj. 'If you hadn't told me, I wouldn't
have known.' [rukan_interlinear p.278 n.27]; Manta xib'e, manta xb'anatäj. 'Si no me hubiera
ido, no habría sucedido.' [oxlajuj p.16].

**Drift: manta and xa ta.** The sequel reserves manta for negative counterfactuals and uses xa
ta for positive ones: Xa ta ïn b'eyom, ninlöq' jun nuch'ich'. 'If I were rich, I'd buy a car.'
[rukan_interlinear p.287 n.91]. La ütz awäch? (Table 8.116, p.194) uses manta for a positive
contrary-to-fact, and the sequel itself slips once (p.119). Oxlajuj Aj combines them: Xa ta xa
rïn in b'eyom, k'o ta k'ïy nuch'ich'. 'Si yo fuera rico, tendría muchos carros.' [oxlajuj p.16].
Context decides; prefer xa ta for positive "if only", manta for "if not".

Also: La ütz awäch? pp.40-41, 199; OKMA Gramática scan pp.336-345.

## 14. Questions and the particle wi

**Models get wrong:** leaving out wi after a fronted place/time/manner phrase; using achike for
'who did it' with an active verb (the agent question needs the antipassive, section 15); inventing
question words; ending yes/no questions with a particle instead of starting with la.

**Rule.** Yes/no questions start with la (optional; intonation alone also works [oxlajuj p.14]).
Content questions front the question word. When the fronted phrase is a place (and often time
or manner), the particle wi follows the verb: "las preguntas con akuchi' llevan la partícula wi
después del verbo, o la partícula existencial k'o" [oxlajuj p.14]. The same wi appears when any
locative is fronted in a statement or relative clause.

| word | meaning | example | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| la | yes/no | La xab'e pa rochoch Ajaw pa nimaq'a'? 'Did you go to church this morning?' | [rukan_interlinear p.278 n.29] |
| achike | what, who, which | Achike xukëm ri Ixmaluk? 'What did Ixmaluk weave?' | [rukan_interlinear p.226 n.46] |
| achike (agent) | who (did it) | ¿Achike xkemon? '¿Quién lo tejió?' | [oxlajuj p.14] |
| akuchi' ... wi | where | Akuchi' yab'e wi? 'Where are you going?' | [rukan_interlinear p.284 n.46] |
| akuchi' ... wi | where | Akuchi' xalöq' wi ri axajab'? 'Where did you buy your sandals?' | [rukan_interlinear p.284 n.48] |
| jampe | when; how many | ¿Jampe xatoqa? '¿Cuándo llegaste?' | [oxlajuj p.14] |
| achike ramaj | at what time | ¿Achike ramaj yojel? '¿A qué hora salimos?' | [oxlajuj p.14] |
| achike roma / ruma | why | ¿Achike ruma man xab'e ta? '¿Por qué no fuiste?' | [oxlajuj p.14] |
| jarupe' | how much | ¿Jarupe' rajïl? '¿Cuánto cuesta?' | [oxlajuj p.15] |
| achike rub'eyal | how | ¿Achike rub'eyal natz'ib'aj? '¿Cómo se escribe?' | [oxlajuj p.14] |
| achoj- + RN | whose, with whom | ¿Achojichin re q'ijob'äl re'? '¿De quién es este reloj?' | [oxlajuj p.15] |

wi in answers and statements: Pa k'ayib'äl xinlöq wi. 'I bought it in the market.'
[rukan_interlinear p.284 n.49]; Wawe' xinaläx wi. 'I was born here.' [rukan_interlinear p.273
n.7]; Wetaman akuchi' öj k'o wi. 'I know where we are.' [rukan_interlinear p.286 n.75].

Word order: VERB + wi/ta + DIRECTIONAL: Xinmestaj akuchi' xinya' wi kan nuparab'äl jöb'. 'I forgot
where I left my umbrella.' [rukan_interlinear p.286 n.74]. (The sequel's prose says only ta may
come between a verb and its directional [rukan p.230], but its own example 74 has wi there; the
2006 course agrees with the example.)

**Variation.** jampe vs jarupe' for 'how many/much' varies by town: "Unos pueblos usan jampe para
¿cuánto? Otros usan: jaru, janipe'" [oxlajuj p.15]. achike modo is common colloquially
[oxlajuj p.14].

Also: La ütz awäch? pp.199-200; OKMA Gramática scan pp.350-353.

## 15. Word order, agent focus and clefts

**Models get wrong:** assuming SVO is the only order; fronting the agent of a "who" question
with an active verb (*Achike xulöq'?* for 'who bought it?'); missing ja ri ... ri in clefts.

**Rule.** The old basic order is Verb-Object-Subject; younger speakers mostly use a Spanish-like
SVO: "In the 1960s and 1970s, the basic word order for Kaqchikel was Verb- Object- Subject. Any
movement of the subject to a position before the verb required that the verb be in antipassive
voice" [rukan p.226]. Today fronting a subject in a plain statement no longer forces the
antipassive, but two constructions still do: questioning the agent, and clefts ("It was X who
...") [rukan p.228].

| Kaqchikel | gloss | source |
|---|---|---|
| Xulöq' kaxlan wäy ri ala'. | The boy bought bread. (VOS) | [rukan_interlinear p.281 n.9] |
| Xutz'ub'aj rumetz' ri xtän. | The young woman kissed her sweetheart. OR Her (sweetheart kissed her) | [rukan_interlinear p.281 n.11] |
| Rumetz' xutz'ub'aj ri xtän. | Her sweetheart kissed the young woman. | [rukan_interlinear p.282 n.14] |
| Achike xulöq' ri ala'? | What did the youth buy? | [rukan_interlinear p.227 n.48] |
| Achike xloq'on? | Who bought it? | [rukan_interlinear p.227 n.49] |
| Ja ri wati't ri xb'in. | It was my grandmother who said (it). | [rukan_interlinear p.227 n.51] |
| Ja ri rumetz' xtz'ub'an richin ri xtän. | It was her sweetheart who kissed the young (woman) | [rukan_interlinear p.282 n.13] |
| Ja ri nata' ri xch'akon. | It was my father who won. | [rukan_interlinear p.284 n.45] |

VOS with two third-person nouns is ambiguous (n.11 has two readings); context, animacy and the
fronted/cleft options resolve it. A fronted object needs no special verb: Jun jeb'ël su't xukëm
ri xtän '(It was) a lovely cloth (that) the young woman wove.' [rukan_interlinear p.284 n.40].

**Drift.** La ütz awäch? (pp.182-183) still requires the focus antipassive whenever the agent is
fronted in clefts and relatives and marks the patient as a plain NP; the sequel requires it only
for agent questions and clefts, and clefts the patient with richin ("Ja ri Ixnal ri xkemon richin
ri jeb'ël pa's." [rukan_interlinear p.225 n.43]). The change is in progress among speakers under
fifty [rukan p.226].

Also: OKMA Gramática scan pp.263-264.

## 16. Voice: passive, antipassive, middle

**Models get wrong:** forming passives with an auxiliary (*x-ux b'an*) or Spanish "fue";
using the wrong suffix for the verb class; keeping Set A on a passive or antipassive (both are
intransitive and take Set B only); expressing the agent with "ruk'in" instead of ruma/roma.

**Rule.** Kaqchikel has "four principal voices: active, passive, antipassive and mediopassive"
[rukan p.220]. All non-active voices are intransitive: aspect + Set B + stem.

- Passive: patient becomes subject; agent optional with -oma/-uma 'by'. Derived -Vj > -Vx with a
  lax vowel; root transitives tense their vowel (xinlöq' > xloq', xutz'ët > xtz'et, xukëm >
  xkem); roots in ' do not change; some speakers add -Vx to roots too (-tz'ët > -tz'etöx)
  [rukan pp.221-222], but texts write the plain xtz'et (16 sources).
- Antipassive: agent stays subject (now in Set B), patient dropped, put in a richin phrase, or
  left bare as a generic object. Derived -Vj > -Vn; roots add -on (-un after u) [rukan pp.223-224].
- Middle -(i)täj: event with no agent implied, "the window got broken" [rukan p.228].

| Kaqchikel | gloss | source |
|---|---|---|
| Xtz'ib'äx ri wuj ruma Humberto Ak'abal. | The book was written by Humberto Ak'ab'al. | [rukan_interlinear p.221 n.32] |
| Xe'ilïx kuma kati't, kimama'. | They were cared for by their grandparents'. | [rukan_interlinear p.221 n.35] |
| Xojq'etëx | We were hugged | [rukan p.221] |
| xojto' | We were helped | [rukan p.222] |
| Xk'utüx ruma ri achin. | Fue pedida por el hombre. | [oxlajuj p.50] |
| Xixb'eloq'on Armita. | Y'all went shopping in Guatemala City. | [rukan_interlinear p.223 n.42] |
| xiq'eten | I hugged (people) | [rukan p.223] |
| Rija' nik'ayin ichaj. | She sells herbs. | [rukan_interlinear p.227 n.55] |
| Rije' yetikon awän. | They plant cornfields. | [rukan_interlinear p.227 n.57] |
| Ri samajel xchoyon richin ri che' pa k'echelaj. | El trabajador cortó el palo en el bosque. | [oxlajuj p.49] |
| Xpaxitäj ri lemow. | The window got shattered. | [rukan p.228] |
| Achike xb'anatäj? | What happened? Literally, what got made? | [rukan_interlinear p.228 n.64] |

The "incorporating antipassive" (verb + bare generic object) names habits and trades: nikemon
po't 'She weaves po'ts. (She is a po't-weaver.)', and gives compound agent nouns with -oy:
kemoy po't 'po't-weaver', b'anoy si' 'woodcutter' [rukan pp.225-226]. Agent nouns in -el are
built on the antipassive: b'ixanel 'singer', tz'ib'anel 'writer'; tijoxel 'student' ("one who is
taught") is built on the passive [rukan pp.238-239].

A fossil instrumental voice in -b'ej survives in a few verbs such as loq'b'ej 'buy something
with X', but the instrument now goes in a ruk'in phrase [rukan p.229].

Also: La ütz awäch? pp.177-183; OKMA Gramática scan pp.299-310.

## 17. Perfects (participles)

**Models get wrong:** treating -näq / -on as past tense with an aspect prefix (*xb'enäq*); using
verbal Set A in- instead of nominal nu- on transitive perfects (*inb'anon* for nub'anon);
translating them only as English present perfect.

**Rule.** Intransitive perfect: stem + -inäq/-enäq/-näq, subject as a free Set B word (ïn, at,
0, öj, ïx, e). Transitive perfect: possessive Set A (nu-, a-, ru-, ...) + root + -on/-un, or a
derived stem with -Vj > -Vn; a non-3s object stands before it as a free Set B word. "Unlike
participles in English or in Spanish they are not rooted in time" [rukan p.218], and they take
no aspect prefix.

| Kaqchikel | gloss | source |
|---|---|---|
| Man ïn warinäq ta. | I'm not asleep. I'm not sleeping. I haven't slept. I hadn't slept. | [rukan_interlinear p.218 n.20] |
| E nutz'eton. | I have seen them. I had seen them. I see them. | [rukan_interlinear p.218 n.21] |
| Öj petenäq Pan Q'än. | We are coming from Antigua Guatemala. We have come from ... | [rukan_interlinear p.218 n.22] |
| Töq xe'oqa ri ula'i', k'a ïn kosinäq pe. | When the guests arrived, I was already tired. | [rukan_interlinear p.218 n.23] |
| Ma ja ni nuloq'on wokem. | I haven't yet bought my ticket. | [rukan_interlinear p.275 n.36] |
| Ïx qak'ulun | We have met y'all. | [rukan p.208] |

The most frequent transitive perfect is -etaman 'know' (from -etamaj 'learn'): wetaman 'yo sé'
[oxlajuj p.11]; Wetaman ruwäch ri xtän. 'I know the young woman.' [rukan_interlinear p.285 n.64].
'Not yet' + perfect: Majani ïn waynäq pe. 'I haven't eaten yet.' [rukan_interlinear p.279 n.53].

Spelling: the sequel writes both kosinäq and kosnäq (epenthesis is optional [rukan p.210]).

Also: La ütz awäch? p.166; OKMA Gramática scan pp.32, 51, 169.

## 18. Complex sentences with two verbs: want, can, must, go-and-do

**Models get wrong:** using an infinitive after "want/can" (Kaqchikel inflects both verbs);
inventing *ninwajo' b'anïk*; forgetting that the second verb agrees with its own subject;
missing the movement + purpose fusion (xb'entz'eta').

**Rule.** -ajo' 'want', -tikïr 'can', and many others take a second fully inflected verb,
usually incompletive, with or without chi. k'o chi + incompletive = 'must, have to'. nik'atzin
chi 'it is necessary that'.

| Kaqchikel | gloss | source |
|---|---|---|
| Ninwajo' nintïj, tab'ana' utzil! | (our tr.: 'I want to eat it, please!') | [rukan p.68] |
| Ninwajo' nintz'ët ri taq juyu' ... | (our tr.: 'I want to see the mountains ...') | [oxlajuj p.78] |
| A Sinakan nrajo' netz'an ruk'in ri moloj ri xch'akon junab'ïr. | Sinakan wants to play (with the team that won last year) | [rukan_interlinear p.286 n.69] |
| Man xitikïr ta yinel jukumaj. | I couldn't leave early. | [rukan_interlinear p.277 n.4] |
| Töq yiyakatäj pa q'ij chïk, k'o chi yichowe. | When I get up late, I have to hurry. | [rukan_interlinear p.286 n.71] |
| Rune'y ri Ixpalow chanin xretamaj nb'iyin. | Ixpalow's child quickly learned to walk. | [rukan_interlinear p.276 n.44] |
| Niqa chi nuwäch yib'e'anin pa taq nimaq'a'. | I like running in the mornings. | [rukan_interlinear p.275 n.32] |

Different subjects take chi: Tab'ana' utzil, nawajo' chi yojb'e pa k'ayb'äl? (our tr.: 'Please,
would you like us to head to the market?') [rukan p.38].

"Like": niqa chi nuwäch 'it pleases me, I like it' (literally 'it falls to my face');
negative Man niqa ta chi qawäch 'We don't like it.' [rukan_interlinear p.259 n.55].

**Go/come to do (incorporated movement).** -b'e 'go' or -pe 'come' fuses with a following verb,
which takes a final -a' (transitive) or nothing (intransitive): Xib'ewa' ruk'in xta Sara. 'I went
to eat with Sara.' [rukan_interlinear p.285 n.56]; Xb'e'intz'eta' > xb'entz'eta' 'I went to see'
and Xo'intz'eta' > xontz'eta' 'I came to see' [rukan_interlinear p.209 n.1-2]; further reduced to
X'entz'eta', Xentz'eta' [rukan p.210]. Oxlajuj Aj lists yib'ewär, yib'exajon (our tr.: 'I go to sleep,
I go to dance') [oxlajuj p.128].

**Drift.** After -tikïr and -ajo', La ütz awäch? shows only incompletive second verbs; the sequel
also has completive + completive (man xitikïr ta xinb'än). Teach incompletive as default.
Venitive (come-to-do): the sequel has o- (xontz'eta'); the OKMA grammar has ul-/to- forms;
treat them as regional.

## 19. Positionals

**Models get wrong:** using an ordinary verb or Spanish "estar sentado" pattern for posture;
not knowing the stative forms (tz'uyül, pa'äl) exist; *xtz'uy* for 'sat down' (it is xtz'uye').

**Rule.** Positional roots (k'ojlemal) name posture, shape and arrangement. They are not
verbs by themselves; each root has a family: a stative in -VC (copying the root vowel, lax) used
as a non-verbal predicate; an intransitive in -e' 'get into the position'; a transitive in
-(V)b'a' 'put into the position'; and descriptive adjectives by partial reduplication + -ïk
(singular) / -äq (plural): "The reduplication takes the form //-V1C1-//" [rukan p.265].

| Kaqchikel | gloss | source |
|---|---|---|
| Ïn k'awäl. | I'm lying supine. | [rukan_interlinear p.270 n.1] |
| Ïx xukül. | Y'all are kneeling. | [rukan_interlinear p.270 n.6] |
| E chupül. | They are extinguished. | [rukan_interlinear p.270 n.8] |
| Ïn pa'äl. | I'm standing up. | [rukan_interlinear p.282 n.22] |
| Töq xtz'uye', xretz' ruwex. | When he sat down, his pants ripped. | [rukan_interlinear p.251 n.27] |

## 20. Relational nouns (Kaqchikel's prepositions)

**Models get wrong:** using Spanish prepositions (con, para, de); leaving relational nouns
unpossessed (*uk'in nute'*); *ruk'in rïn* for 'with me' (it is wuk'in / wik'in); confusing chi
re (dative) and richin (for, of); using pa for 'with'.

**Rule.** Kaqchikel has only a few true prepositions (pa 'in, at, to', chi 'at, to'). Everything
else is a relational noun: a noun that must be possessed by the thing it relates. "Los
sustantivos relacionales son siempre poseídos" [oxlajuj p.49]. The possessor prefix agrees with
the object of the relation; a full noun follows: ruk'in rute' 'with his mother'.

| relational noun | meaning | example | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| -ichin | possession, purpose, antipassive patient | Xqalöq' ri ti'ïj richin ri wa'in. 'Compramos la carne para la comida.' | [oxlajuj p.50] |
| -oma / -uma | because of; passive agent | Awuma rat xinkich'äy. 'Me pegaron por tu culpa.' | [oxlajuj p.50] |
| -uk'in / -ik'in | with (company, instrument) | Xkib'e awuk'in. 'I'll go with you.' | [rukan_interlinear p.246 n.15] |
| chi -e | to (dative, recipient) | Xusipaj jun kab' chwe. 'She gave a candy to me.' | [rukan_interlinear p.247 n.18] |
| -i' | self (reflexive, reciprocal) | Xusök ri'. 'She cut herself.' | [rukan_interlinear p.256 n.34] |
| pa -wi' | on, about | E k'o jujun taq wuj pa ruwi' ri ch'atal. 'There are some books on the table.' | [rukan_interlinear p.237 n.10] |
| chi -wäch | in front of; before (time) | Chwäch nqatïj qawa'im, nqamatyoxij. 'Before we eat our food, we give thanks.' | [rukan_interlinear p.286 n.72] |
| chi -ij | behind; after (time) | Chrij xech'akatäj, xeb'ison. 'After they lost, they were sad.' | [rukan_interlinear p.286 n.73] |
| xe -xikin | beside | Xtz'uye' xe nuxikin. 'She sat beside me.' | [rukan_interlinear p.259 n.52] |
| chi -pam | inside | Chupam jun kaxa xinwïl wi. 'I found it in a box.' | [rukan_interlinear p.284 n.51] |
| chi -kojol | among | ... chi kikojol ri taq ch'umil. '... among the stars.' | [rukan_interlinear p.238 n.14] |

Body-part idioms are relational: Mani katzijon chqij! Tab'ij pe chi qawäch! 'Don't talk behind
our backs! Say it to our face!' [rukan_interlinear p.259 n.53].

The dative is written chi re or contracted chre; plural chike / chke ("chre rute'" [rukan p.221];
"chi ke" [rukan p.29]). Oxlajuj Aj's list adds -chi' 'en la orilla de', -raqän 'al pie de' and
xe' 'debajo de, antes de' [oxlajuj p.49].

**Drift: -oma/-uma and -ik'in/-uk'in.** Speech mostly says roma and rik'in; older texts and the
standardization proposal prefer -uma and -uk'in. Oxlajuj Aj: "La forma más común en el habla es
//-oma//. Pero fuentes escritas antiguas tienen //-uma//. ... La propuesta para estandardización
es //-uma//, a pesar a su poco uso actual" and the same for -ik'in / -uk'in [oxlajuj p.50]. The
sequel's grammar writes ruma and ruk'in, while its own running text writes roma about 490 times
to ruma 25, and rik'in 195 to 47. Either is correct; keep one per text.

Also: La ütz awäch? pp.127, 156; OKMA Gramática scan pp.41, 47, 359-364.

## 21. Directionals and motion

**Models get wrong:** omitting directionals where Kaqchikel expects them (the result reads like
Spanish translated); treating them as prepositions; misplacing them before the verb or before ta.

**Rule.** Directionals (k'utk'ajtzij) are particles from motion verbs, placed after the verb:
ok (enter), el (leave), qa (descend), pe (hither), apo (thither), kan (remain), plus the adverb
aqanïj (up), which is not from a verb [rukan pp.230-233]. Spellings of 'up': aqanäj (2 sources /
4 tokens), aqanij (1 / 3), aqanïj (1 / 1). "only the irrealis //ta// may intervene between them and the verb"
[rukan p.230] (wi may too; see section 14). pe is toward the speaker or main character, apo away
from them, "much like hither and thither did in English" [rukan p.231]. They also carry aspect:
qa 'slowly, completely', el 'suddenly, starting off', kan 'for good, from now on', pe
'beginning', ok 'affection, smallness' [rukan pp.231-233].

| Kaqchikel | gloss | source |
|---|---|---|
| Xok ok pa rujul ri umül. | The rabbit entered into its hole. | [rukan_interlinear p.230 n.67] |
| Ri yuquch'ich' xel el pa tz'aqät ramaj. | The train pulled out on time. | [rukan_interlinear p.230 n.68] |
| Xkinapon apo iwuk'in pa lajuj ramaj. | I'll arrive there where y'all are at 10 o'clock. | [rukan_interlinear p.230 n.71] |
| Tab'ij pe chwe. | Tell me. | [rukan_interlinear p.255 n.30] |
| Xsamäj qa. | He worked slowly, meticulously. | [rukan_interlinear p.232 n.75] |
| Töq xq'ajan ri q'aq', xojchäp el chi anin. | When the gun went off, we started out running. | [rukan_interlinear p.232 n.74] |
| Taya' kan apeqës chi la'! | Leave your backpack over there! | [rukan_interlinear p.273 n.8] |

Motion verbs: -b'e 'go', -pe 'come', -apon 'arrive there', -oqa 'arrive here', -tzolin 'return',
-el 'leave', -ok 'enter', -qa 'descend', -jote' 'go up'. Destination takes pa or chi, or nothing
before a place name: Xb'e Armita jukumaj. 'He went to Guatemala City early.'
[rukan_interlinear p.273 n.11]; Chwa'q xkojb'e chi palow. 'Tomorrow we will go to the beach.'
[rukan_interlinear p.273 n.13].

The sequel notes that younger speakers schooled in Spanish drop directionals, and that teachers
once marked Maya students down for Spanish calques of them ("entraron para adentro") [rukan
p.231]. Use them.

Also: La ütz awäch? pp.141, 189; OKMA Gramática scan pp.122-124.

## 22. Reflexive and reciprocal

**Models get wrong:** using Spanish "se"; marking the object with a matching Set B (*xinin-*);
forgetting that -i' agrees with the subject.

**Rule.** The verb is an ordinary transitive with a zero 3s object; after it comes the relational
noun -i' carrying the subject's own person: wi', awi', ri', qi', iwi', ki'. "El
poseedor siempre concuerda con el sujeto del verbo" [oxlajuj p.80]. With a plural subject it is
also reciprocal.

| Kaqchikel | gloss | source |
|---|---|---|
| xinsokaj wi' | me corté | [oxlajuj p.80] |
| xusokaj ri' | se cortó | [oxlajuj p.80] |
| xqasokaj qi' | nos cortamos | [oxlajuj p.80] |
| Xoq' ruma xuxib'ij ri'. | She cried because she was scared. Literally, she cried because she frightened herself. | [rukan_interlinear p.247 n.16] |
| Xtiqatz'eta' qi' ke la'. | We'll see each other there. | [rukan_interlinear p.216 n.12] |
| Nikich'anab'a' ki' töq yetujan. | They take their clothes off when they take (a sweatbath) | [rukan_interlinear p.271 n.27] |

Many everyday verbs are lexically reflexive: -tijoj ri' 'study' (Ri jeb'ël xtän nutijoj ri' pa
nimatijob'äl. 'The lovely girl is studying at the university.' [rukan_interlinear p.270 n.12]),
-kuqub'a' ranima 'calm down', -molo' ki' 'meet' (Tiqamolo' qi' we luq'ij we maq'ij. 'Let's meet
either Monday or Tuesday.' [rukan_interlinear p.286 n.82]).

**Drift.** Xtiqatz'eta' qi' puts -a' on a potential root transitive before qi'; the sequel's own
xtikimöl ki' and the 2006 course's xkik'ül ki' have no suffix. Either occurs.

Also: La ütz awäch? p.124; OKMA Gramática scan pp.254-255.

## 23. Causatives and other verb derivation

**Models get wrong:** forming "make someone X" with a Spanish-style auxiliary (*nub'än chi
nwär*); inventing causative shapes.

**Rule.** -(V)saj makes a transitive "cause to X" from an intransitive. Versives -Vr 'become X'
come from adjectives and some nouns, and take -isaj in turn. Sound changes are lexical: -wär >
-wartisaj, -b'iyin > -b'inisaj, -el > -elesaj [oxlajuj p.86].

| base | derived | source |
|---|---|---|
| -el salir | -elesaj sacar, hacer salir | [oxlajuj p.86] |
| -wär dormirse | -wartisaj | [oxlajuj p.86] |
| -ok entrar | -okisaj | [oxlajuj p.86] |
| nïm grande | -nimïr engrandecerse > -nimirisaj engrandecer | [oxlajuj pp.85-86] |
| tew frío | -tewïr enfriarse | [oxlajuj p.85] |
| q'än amarillo | -q'anär amarillarse, madurarse | [oxlajuj p.85] |

Examples:
- Xinwartisaj ri ne'y. 'I put the baby to sleep.' [rukan p.220]
- Xrelesaj rub'uküt pa ruchi' jay. 'She took off her shoes at the door.' [rukan_interlinear p.273 n.9]
- Man takamisaj jun winäq. 'Thou shalt not kill.' (-kam 'die' > -kamisaj 'kill') [rukan_interlinear p.279 n.57]

"La vocal relajada del sufijo [versivo] no es predecible, auque la vocal más frecuente es ï"
[oxlajuj p.85]: check each versive in a dictionary. Other derivations: -Vj transitives from
nouns, -Vn antipassives, -el agent nouns, -b'äl instrument/place nouns (tz'ib'ab'äl 'pen',
tijob'äl 'school'), -ïk action nouns (tijonïk 'class', k'ulub'ïk 'wedding') [rukan p.242].

Also: La ütz awäch? p.174; OKMA Gramática scan pp.161, 253.

## 24. Plurals

**Models get wrong:** pluralizing every noun (*tz'i'a'*, *wuji'*); missing taq; pluralizing
adjectives that do not inflect; ignoring that verb agreement already shows plurality.

**Rule.** "Most nouns do not inflect for plural. Plurality can be shown through numbers,
adjectives, or verbal inflection" [rukan p.237]. A small set, mostly people, take -i' or -a'
(-a' "after roots or stems with an /i/ in the last syllable" [rukan p.237]); the lax vowel then
tenses. taq before a noun is a free plural marker. Agentive -el nouns all pluralize
(tijonel > tijonela').

| singular | plural | source |
|---|---|---|
| ak'wal | ak'wala' | [rukan p.237] |
| xtän | xtani' | [rukan p.237] |
| ala' | alab'oni' | [rukan p.238] |
| ixöq | ixoqi' | [rukan p.238] |
| achin | achi'a' | [rukan p.238] |
| winäq | winaqi' | [oxlajuj p.13] |
| tijoxel | tijoxela' | [oxlajuj p.11] |
| ne'y | taq ne'y | [oxlajuj p.13] |
| nim 'big' | nima'q | [rukan p.265] |

Examples: Xkitz'ët ka'i' xik'. (two falcons, no plural on xik') [rukan_interlinear p.237 n.11];
Ri ajyuq' xeruyuq'uj rukarne'l. 'The shepherd herds his sheep.' (plural only in xe-)
[rukan_interlinear p.237 n.12]; Setesäq ri taq läq. 'The plates are round.' [rukan_interlinear
p.271 n.32].

Worldview matters: a star (ch'umil) "viewed as a powerful spiritual entity ... inflects for
plural: ch'umila'"; as a ball of gas it does not [rukan p.238]. ne'y 'baby' does not pluralize
for most speakers [rukan p.239].

**Drift.** aj- agentives: the sequel says only ajq'ij pluralizes (ajq'ija') [rukan p.239]; La
ütz awäch? (Table 8.30, p.152) pluralizes them freely. Family terms: the sequel says most do not
pluralize (taq tata'aj, *tataji') except te'ej > teja' [rukan p.240]; La ütz awäch? says almost
all person nouns do.

Also: La ütz awäch? pp.150-152; OKMA Gramática scan pp.48, 53.

## 25. Subordination: when, that, so that, if, relative clauses

**Models get wrong:** using Spanish que/cuando/porque; using an infinitive for purpose without
richin; forgetting wi in locative relatives.

**Rule.** Clauses are joined by particles and relational nouns, and subordinate verbs are
fully inflected.

| link | use | example | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| töq / toq | when | Töq xtz'uye', xretz' ruwex. 'When he sat down, his pants ripped.' | [rukan_interlinear p.251 n.27] |
| chi | that (complement) | Xub'ij chi man nrajo' ta nb'e. 'She said she didn't want to go.' | [rukan_interlinear p.286 n.78] |
| chi | that | Ninnojij chi k'o jun k'ak'a' rumetz'. 'I think he has a new sweetheart.' | [rukan_interlinear p.286 n.79] |
| ri | relative 'who, which' | Wetaman ruwäch ri xtän ri xub'ixaj ri b'ix "Tukr". 'I know the young woman who sang ...' | [rukan_interlinear p.285 n.65] |
| ri | relative | Ri achin ri xb'e Pa Jotöl nutäq pe pwäq chre ri rixjayil. 'The man who went to the (US sends money to his wife)' | [rukan_interlinear p.285 n.66] |
| richin + -ik | in order to | Xuköl kan rupwaq richin ruloqik jun ch'ich'. 'He saved his money to buy a car.' | [rukan_interlinear p.258 n.49] |
| richin + -ik | in order to | Ninkanuj jun xtän richin ruchajik nune'y. 'I'm looking for a girl to tend my infant.' | [rukan_interlinear p.258 n.48] |
| richin (+ finite) | so that | Xkilöq' k'ïy xupxup richin nkinimaq'ijuj ruq'ij ri kalk'wal. | [rukan_interlinear p.257 n.46] |
| ruma / roma | because | Ruma man xwär ta toq'a', xwär qa chupam rutijonik. 'Because he didn't sleep at night, he fell asleep in class.' | [rukan_interlinear p.257 n.44] |
| wi | if (real) | Wi natz'ët ri Ma Xwan, tab'ana' utzil tab'ij chre chi woyob'en. 'If you see Xwan, (please tell him I'm waiting)' | [rukan_interlinear p.287 n.87] |
| xa ta / manta | if (counterfactual) | see section 13 | |
| chwäch / chrij | before / after | Chrij ri wa'im, xtiqachäp samaj. 'After the meal, we'll start working.' | [rukan_interlinear p.275 n.33] |
| we ... we / wi ... wi | either ... or | Wi A Bartolo wi A Max ja ri ch'ip. 'Either Bartolo or Max is the last-born child.' | [rukan_interlinear p.286 n.83] |

The -ik form is a possessed verbal noun (ruloqik 'its buying'), so its possessor names the
object or subject: ruchajik nune'y 'tending my baby'. The sequel also shows chi as a
relativizer (Ri achin chi xsamäj pa ri awän, kosnäq xtzolin pa rochoch. [rukan_interlinear p.286
n.81]); ri is more common.

Coordination: chuqa' 'and, also'; po 'but'; xa ja ri' 'however'; o 'or'; i 'and' between nouns
(Markos i Estela xekule' iwïr. 'Markos and Estela got married yesterday.' [rukan_interlinear
p.285 n.59]); ruk'in 'with' can mean 'and' (Xkitïk awän Ma Lu ruk'in ruk'ajol. 'Lu and his son
planted corn.' [rukan_interlinear p.284 n.54]). Note the plural verb with a singular subject +
ruk'in: Xojb'ewa' ruk'in xta Sara. 'I went to eat with Sara.' (lit. 'we went') [rukan_interlinear
p.285 n.57].

**Drift.** "before (in time)": La ütz awäch? uses chi ruxe' / xuxe'; the sequel uses chwäch and
chrij [rukan p.286]. Both are current.

Also: La ütz awäch? p.233; OKMA Gramática scan pp.368, 388.

## 26. Adjectives, intensifiers and comparison

**Models get wrong:** inventing a comparative suffix (*nimër*); using Spanish más as the only
option; stacking adjectives before a noun; forgetting the -(V)läj intensifier.

**Rule.** Adjectives go before the noun by preference (Ri jeb'ël xtän [rukan_interlinear p.262
n.1]), though they may follow (Jun achin q'or [rukan_interlinear p.262 n.4]). "Kaqchikel does
not have a derived comparative. Comparisons are made syntactically and often inferred from
context" [rukan p.266]. The standard is marked with chwäch / chuwäch 'in front of', or the
better one is focused with ja ... ri. Intensifiers: janila, yalan 'very', -(V)läj 'very, really'
(utz > utziläj, q'or > q'oraläj), and repetition (räx räx 'very green') [rukan pp.266-267].
Spanish más is often borrowed [rukan p.266].

| Kaqchikel | gloss | source |
|---|---|---|
| Rija' nim chwäch ri wati't. | She is older than my grandmother. | [rukan p.266] |
| Ri Lexus jotöl rajil chwäch ri Toyota. | A Lexus is more expensive than a Toyota. | [rukan p.266] |
| Ja ri xta Marta ri janila nim. | Marta is the taller. | [rukan p.266] |
| Tab'ana' utzil kab'iyin jub'a mas eqal. | Please walk a little slower. | [rukan p.266] |
| Utziläj ana'oj! | Great idea! | [rukan p.267] |
| Yalan jotöl rajïl. Taqasaj! | It's very expensive. Lower (the asking price)! | [rukan_interlinear p.276 n.55] |

Adjective as verb: versives (section 23) and Xjeb'e' wakamïn. 'She became beautiful today.'
[rukan_interlinear p.271 n.23]. Abstract nouns: jeb'ël > rujeb'elen 'its beauty', räx > ruraxal
'its greenness' [rukan p.207].

Also: La ütz awäch? pp.164-165; OKMA Gramática scan pp.165-166.

## 27. Numbers, counting and time

**Models get wrong:** decimal Maya numbers (*ka'i' lajuj* for 20); inventing forms above 20;
using Maya numbers where speakers use Spanish (prices, clock times, years); forgetting that
numbers precede the noun with no plural needed.

**Rule.** The system is vigesimal. 1-10 (with Oxlajuj Aj's Spanish): jun uno, ka'i' dos, oxi' tres,
kaji' cuatro, wo'o' cinco, waqi' seis, wuqu' siete, waqxaqi' ocho, b'eleje' nueve, lajuj diez; 11-19 combine a unit stem with -lajuj (julajuj, kab'lajuj, oxlajuj ...); 20
juk'al (also juwinäq), 40 kak'al, 60 oxk'al, 80 kajk'al, 100 wok'al, 400 juq'o' [oxlajuj p.25].
Numbers between scores count into the next score: 21-39 'N of its second twenty' (N rukak'al),
41-59 'N of its third twenty' (N roxk'al). Oxlajuj Aj names its own lessons this way: "Ka'i'
rukak'al Tijonïk" (lesson 22), "Jun roxk'al Tijonïk" (lesson 41), "Lajuj roxk'al Tijonïk" (lesson
50) [oxlajuj pp.48, 71, 82]. Ordinals: nab'ey 'primero', ruka'n 'segundo', rox 'tercero', rukaj,
ro', ruwaq, ruwuq, ruwaqxaq, rub'elej, rulaj [oxlajuj p.25].

Juk'al "otros se usan -winäq solo para contar días y/o personas" [oxlajuj p.108]: Röj öj juwinäq
winaqi'. (our tr.: 'We are twenty people.') [oxlajuj p.34].

Time: q'ij 'sun, day', ik' 'moon, month', juna' 'year', ramaj 'hour, time', wuqq'ij 'week';
iwïr 'yesterday', chwa'q 'tomorrow', kab'ij 'day after tomorrow', kab'ijïr 'two days ago'
[rukan p.209]; apo after a time phrase points to the future, kan to the past: Ka'i' juna' apo
xkojb'e Mexiko. 'We will go to Mexico in two years.' [rukan_interlinear p.274 n.16]; ka'i'
wuqq'ij kan 'two weeks ago' [rukan_interlinear p.272 n.40]. Repetition = 'every': Q'ij q'ij
nuk'utuj rurtisanik rute'. 'She asks for her mother's blessing daily.' [rukan_interlinear p.274
n.19]; Juna' juna' yeb'e Esquipulas. 'They go to Esquipulas every year.' [rukan_interlinear
p.274 n.23]. Clock time: lajuj ramaj '10 o'clock' [rukan_interlinear p.230 n.71], often Spanish.

Calendar names: men take day + number (Jun No'j, Oxlajuj Ajpu'), women Ix- + day (Ixno'j)
[rukan pp.234-235]. Full tables in paradigms.md.

Also: La ütz awäch? pp.41-44; OKMA Gramática scan pp.77-79.

## 28. Quick checklist before you output Kaqchikel

1. Every finite verb has an aspect prefix, and the prefix agrees with the Set B (n-/y-, xt-/xk-,
   t-/k-).
2. Intransitive subject = Set B; transitive subject = Set A; object = Set B, placed first.
3. 3s Set A is u- right after an aspect prefix, ru- after an object prefix, r- before vowels.
4. No copula: Set B word + noun/adjective (ïn tijonel). k'o only for existence, location, having.
5. Lax vowels only in the last syllable, and they tense when a suffix is added or a class-2 noun
   is possessed.
6. Root transitives take -V' in the imperative; derived -Vj verbs do not.
7. "Who did it?" takes the antipassive (Achike xloq'on?).
8. Negation is man ... ta around the predicate; manäq / majun for "none".
9. Prepositions are relational nouns, possessed: ruk'in, richin, chre, chuwäch, pa ruwi'.
10. Fronted place phrases take wi after the verb.
11. Most nouns do not pluralize; use taq, numbers or verb agreement.
12. Prefer attested forms (paradigms.md); mark anything derived but unwitnessed.
